# Extraction quality and leakage findings

Codex extracted 14,090 feature values from 1,409 previews in 57 batches. Local checks confirmed record coverage, allowed categories, and the presence of supporting quotes. Automated evidence filtering changed 13 values to `unknown` when their quotes were absent from the matching preview and cleared four quotes associated with already unknown values. The full extraction used `gpt-6.1-sol` at low reasoning effort with compact batches of up to 25 previews. Independent human annotation was omitted, so this run did not measure the accuracy of individual interpretations. [Extraction summary](extraction_summary.json) · [Run protocol](../../../docs/FULL_RUN_PROTOCOL.md)

Codex agreed with its original output on **243 of 250 feature values (97.2%)** when the same 25 training-season previews were repeated in the same order, with the same prompt and settings. This measures consistency for one batch, and frequent unknown values can raise agreement. [Repeat agreement](repeat_agreement.json)

In the separate metadata-only diagnostic, Codex correctly returned **279 of 279** outcomes. It matched all 132 home wins, 71 draws, and 76 away wins. The diagnostic inputs comprised record IDs, home and away team names, and match dates. All three request hashes were reproduced from those fields. The saved predictions matched the raw CLI responses, and the logs showed zero tool calls. On the same fixtures, the recent-results logistic baseline got 140 of 279 correct and the always-home rule got 132. [Diagnostic scores](metadata_diagnostic.json) · [Diagnostic configuration](../../../data/codex_metadata_diagnostic.manifest.json)

The perfect metadata result is strong evidence that Codex had access to historical outcomes through the model or its serving system. The test leaves the source of that knowledge and its use during masked preview extraction unresolved. The diagnostic predictions were kept out of the training features. Treat the preview-feature comparison as exploratory because masked articles can still reveal fixture identities through player names, managers, venues, and match context.
